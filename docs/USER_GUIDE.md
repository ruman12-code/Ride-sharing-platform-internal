# Using Office Carpool

For colleagues, and for whoever administers it. No jargon.

---

## The idea in one paragraph

You are driving to work anyway. Someone in your office is going the same way and
is standing in a queue for a bus. This puts you in touch, works out a fair share
of the fuel, and gets out of the way. **You are sharing the cost of a journey you
were already making — you are not driving for hire, and the app will not let you
charge enough to make it a business.**

---

## Getting in

There is nothing to install and no new password. It opens in Teams and knows who
you are from the sign-in you already have.

You are not asked whether you are a driver or a rider. Most people are both,
depending on the day.

---

## Offering a seat

Tap **Offer a ride**. Four short screens, about half a minute.

### 1 — Route

Type the first few letters of where you are starting from and tap it. Same for
where you are going.

You must pick from the list. There is no free typing of place names, and that is
deliberate: in the old spreadsheet one colleague wrote the same destination four
different ways in five months, and nothing could be matched to anything.

The app then works out the route and shows the stops along it. **Every one of
those stops is somewhere a colleague can get in or out.** If you would rather not
stop somewhere, tap it to remove it.

### 2 — When

Pick a time, and decide whether it repeats.

**Turn on "Repeat weekly" if this is your normal commute.** This is the part
worth understanding, because it is the whole point of the app: set it once and
you will never fill this form in again. Each evening you get a message asking
*"Driving Uttara to Gulshan tomorrow 07:45?"* with a **Yes** button. Tap it and
your seats are live. You never open the app.

If you skip a day, tap **Not tomorrow**. Nothing else happens and nobody is told.

### 3 — Seats and car

How many seats, and what you are driving so people can spot you — model, colour,
last four of the plate. Then any preferences: women only, air conditioning,
luggage room, quiet.

### 4 — Cost share

The app has already worked it out and shows you exactly how:

> 14 km × Tk 16.92/km = Tk 237 fuel ÷ 3 people = Tk 70 each
> You recover Tk 140 of your Tk 237.
> *Based on octane at Tk 145/L (2026-06-01) and 8.6 km/L in Dhaka traffic.*

**You can lower it, or make it free. You cannot raise it.** The slider stops at
the calculated amount and there is no way around that — not in settings, not for
administrators.

The reason is in the arithmetic. The cost is divided by everyone in the car
*including you*, so you always pay a share and always end up out of pocket. It is
not possible to profit, which is what keeps this cost-sharing rather than a taxi
service.

Then check the summary and tap **Publish**.

*Doing it again tomorrow?* Your saved route is the first thing on screen. One tap
takes you straight to the cost share.

## Finding a seat

Tap **Find a ride**. The search is already filled in with your usual commute, so
usually you just tap **Search**.

### Reading the results

Each result shows the driver, their department, the departure time, the cost
share, how far you would walk to the pick-up, and how many seats are left.

The coloured label tells you *why* it matched:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Exact route** | They start and finish exactly where you do |
| **On the way** | You join or leave at one end of their journey |
| **Short detour** | You are both in the middle of their route |

All three are fine. The label is there so nothing is a surprise.

### Asking for a seat

Tap **Request seat**. You will be asked one question:

> **How would you otherwise travel?** Bus · Rickshaw/CNG · My own car ·
> Uber/Pathao · I wouldn't travel

One tap, and it matters. It is the only way the organisation can show this takes
cars off the road rather than emptying buses — and it cannot be worked out later.

Choose how you will settle up, then **Confirm**. The driver gets a message and
can accept from it.

### If nothing matches

That is expected early on, when few people have joined.

Tap **Alert me** and the app tells you the moment a colleague posts that route.
Your search is also recorded — anonymously — so the organisation can see which
routes people want but cannot get.

If you drive that way yourself, there is a button to offer a seat instead.

## Your day, from notifications

You should rarely need to open the app.

| When | What arrives | What you tap |
|---|---|---|
| Evening before | *"Driving Uttara → Gulshan tomorrow 07:45?"* | **Yes** / **Not tomorrow** |
| Someone asks | *"Nusrat wants a seat, boarding at Khilkhet."* | **Accept** / **Decline** |
| A match appears | *"Rezaul is driving your route, Tk 90."* | **Request seat** |
| 45 min before | *"Still on for 07:45?"* | **Yes, on my way** / **Can't make it** |
| After the trip | *"How was it?"* | ★★★★★ |
| Thursday | What you shared, who is driving next week | — |

**Please answer the 45-minute one.** Someone is deciding whether to wait for you.
A no-show is the fastest way to make a colleague stop offering seats.

## Money

**No money moves through this app.** It works out the amount and records who owes
whom. You settle it however you like — cash, or the organisation reimburses it.

Your balance shows as *"You've given 12 rides, taken 4."* It is a picture of
give-and-take, not a bill. Credits cannot be bought and cannot be turned into
cash.

---

## Ratings and problems

After a trip you can leave stars. **Only averages are ever shown — nobody sees
who rated them what.**

The same screen lets you report a problem: a no-show, unsafe driving, or anything
about safety. Reports go to administrators only.

---

## Privacy, briefly

- **No GPS. No location tracking.** Ever.
- Journeys are recorded as neighbourhoods, never street addresses.
- Your phone number is hidden until you and a colleague have both confirmed a
  booking — and every time it is viewed, that is logged.
- Nothing leaves the organisation.
- **Worth knowing:** a regular commute does tell colleagues that you travel from
  a particular area at a particular time. That is unavoidable in carpooling. You
  can pause or delete it instantly.
- Declining is silent. Nobody is told who declined or why.

Everything you can ask for is under **Settings → My data**. Full detail in the
[privacy notice](PRIVACY_NOTICE.md).

## For administrators

Under the **Admin** tab.

### The fuel rate — the one thing to keep an eye on

Every cost share is calculated from the current fuel price. Bangladesh adjusts
fuel prices periodically, so **a rate left unchecked quietly mis-prices every
ride.**

The app tells you when a rate has not been confirmed in 35 days. You then either:

- **"Still correct"** — the gazetted price has not changed. Confirms it, and the
  history stays honest: no price change is invented that never happened.
- **Enter the new rate** — a new dated record. Past rides keep the rate that
  applied on their day, so old cost shares are never rewritten.

**The app ships with this alarm already showing.** That is intentional — the
first thing you should do is confirm the rate yourself, rather than trust a
confirmation somebody else typed in.

### Everything else

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rides per driver per day** | Default 2. Adjustable, never removable |
| **Credit ledger** | Who owes whom. Export for reimbursement |
| **Incidents** | Reports from colleagues, newest first |
| **This week** | Completed trips, rides published, searches that found nothing, car trips avoided |
| **Export to Excel** | Rides, bookings, balances, impact, fuel prices |

### The numbers to actually watch

**Completed trips is the one that counts.** The spreadsheet this replaced could
not establish that a single ride ever happened. One provable completed trip is a
real improvement over that.

**Searches that found nothing** is your demand map. It shows routes colleagues
want and cannot get, which tells you who to go and ask to drive.

The old baseline was **0.185 postings per working day**. That is what to beat.

### The Excel export

Five sheets: Rides, Bookings, Balances, Impact, Fuel prices.

The **Rides** sheet includes each cost share next to the official taxi-guideline
fare for the same distance. If anyone ever asks whether this is an unlicensed
taxi service, that column is the answer: the shares are a fraction of a metered
fare, and they have to be, because the app cannot produce anything higher.

The **Impact** sheet counts only colleagues who would otherwise have driven or
taken Uber/Pathao as cars taken off the road. Bus and rickshaw switchers are
listed but counted as zero — inflating that number would discredit the whole
figure.

Excel is for reporting. It is never where the data lives. That is exactly the
mistake the old workbook made.
